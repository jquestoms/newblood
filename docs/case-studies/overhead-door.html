New Blood designed and built the site for Overhead Door years ago and has hosted and managed it ever since. The original build is not what makes this case study interesting. The fact that the leadership team has not had to think about the site — and has not, for years — is.

The long phase

Once the site was live, the engagement shifted into its long phase: keep it running, keep it current, and keep it from ever becoming something the leadership team has to think about.

The team here is dispatching trucks, scheduling installs, and answering service calls. They are not in the business of thinking about plugin updates, SSL renewals, performance regressions, or whether the contact form is still routing correctly. Our job is to make sure those things never become their problem in the first place.

What we look after

Hosting with consistent uptime, year after year

Their site lives on infrastructure we picked, configured, and have run for them for years. Uptime has been steady through the long stretches that make hosting boring — which is exactly what hosting is supposed to be. When something does need attention, we are the ones who notice and resolve it; the client typically learns about it after the fact, in passing.

WordPress management as a service

WordPress sites that aren't actively cared for tend to drift — plugins go out of date, security patches pile up, conflicts surface, and one day the contact form silently stops sending. We keep the platform current, the plugins compatible, the backups running, and the front-end intact. The site doesn't get edited by accident, and it doesn't break in the night. The owner doesn't have a "WordPress problem" to schedule around, because there isn't one.

Custom notification scripts for service requests

An off-the-shelf form plugin sends an email and calls it done. That wasn't enough for an operation routing thousands of service requests a year across multiple service categories and three regional locations. We wrote custom notification logic that sorts incoming requests, routes them to the right people, and lands in their inbox in a shape the team can act on immediately — no copy-pasting, no triage, no missed jobs because the wrong person opened the email first. Off-menu engineering, written specifically for how this business actually runs.

A real point of contact

When something does need a human — a content change, a new landing page, a question about why a vendor email is bouncing — there is one place to send it and a person who already knows the site. No tickets, no escalations, no relearning the environment every time. That continuity is most of what a managed engagement actually buys you.

A long, quiet relationship

Most of what we do for Overhead Door doesn't make for a dramatic case study. The site renders. The forms route. The hosting runs. The owner thinks about doors and docks. We think about everything else.

A seventy-year-old Mid-Atlantic institution that doesn't have to think about its website is one of the engagements we are proudest of. The work is undramatic on purpose.
