# Repositioning newblood.com around territory-defense engagements
*Working plan, v2, 2026-09-02. Source: OHDBalt win ($35k + $3,200/mo, signed 9/2). Reviewed against the live site and revised the same day; the decisions in "What changed in v2" are settled. Phase-one spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-02-territory-phase-one-design.md`.*

## The core finding

The site sells 7x below the work just sold. The price-modesty signal is not one line but four: the hero subtitle ("more memorable than the budget should allow"), two About paragraphs ("much bigger budget," "even on a small-business budget," "every dollar goes further") plus the "Right-sized budgets" heading, and the pricing page's unnamed "Let's Talk" ceiling above $5,000. The FAQ also still promises delivery "in 1-2 weeks," which contradicts both the deliberative brand rule and the ninety-day shape just sold. The OHDBalt-class buyer (an established operator considering $35,000 plus $3,200/mo, with $90k in view) cannot find themselves anywhere on the site.

What OHDBalt proved is a repeatable playbook worth naming: read their own data (ad platform, inquiry database, search territory) -> four gaps -> platform + content program -> everything owned in their name. That playbook is the product.

## What changed in v2 (decisions)

1. **Craft leads; Territory is the second door.** The hero stays craft + AI. The two doors are the section directly below the hero, not the hero itself. The musical voice keeps its three high-concentration spots (hero, About story, How we arrange a project) untouched.
2. **One audit, one program.** The Four Gaps Audit absorbs Signal as its search-territory reading. The Territory monthly absorbs the Search & AI Visibility retainer (it is the $1,250 content line in the signed agreement). The $3,500 Visibility sweep is retired from the lineup and its sales doc is no longer sent. The About-page Signal intro stays and will point to the Four Gaps page once it exists.
3. **Territory is a package, not a fifth service.** Build, Tune, Manage, Empower remain "what we do." Territory = Build + Manage + the content program, packaged for established operators. Say so on the page.
4. **Business is not demoted.** The two doors are named by buyer situation ("A site that represents you" / "A platform that wins your territory"), never by size. Starter and Business keep their current framing; Business keeps "Popular."
5. **The $90k tier prints its price only after a one-page shape exists.** Nothing in the repo prices Option 3; it was never quoted. Until the shape doc is written, the Operations Platform shows its shape with "scoped per operator."
6. **No anonymized OHDBalt case study.** A named Overhead Door case study already exists (`/case-study-overhead-door/`, currently draft locally) with the same history and job counts; anonymizing next to it is theater. The existing page is rewritten into the platform story at their launch, with permission, relabeled Manage -> Build. Until then the findings live only in Notes articles, unnamed.
7. **Exclusivity is part of the offer.** One operator per trade per market, stated on the Territory page and the later vertical page. It protects OHDBalt and gives a prospect a reason to sign before a competitor does.
8. **Fix the live contradictions first.** The four modesty lines and the FAQ timeline are a one-session fix and go before any new page.

## The moves (revised)

### 1. Territory flagship page (`/territory`)
The productized OHDBalt engagement. Name the buyer plainly: established local operators (trades, distributors, commercial service companies), decades of reputation, watching upstarts and lead-gen middlemen buy the searches their name earned. Carry the motto: *"The content earns the visit. The website earns the call. And everything that earns is yours."* Scope mirrors the signed agreement in generic form: platform, AI intake (types, never talks), save-first forms, reviews engine, spec/resource desk, project highlights, tracked numbers in their name. Ninety-day shape, the four commitments, exclusivity, pricing in two lines, credit line. Authority register, one defensive line per page at most, no musical phrasing beyond one earned word. No client named.

### 2. Pricing page as two doors
Door one: Starter, Business, then the existing "Already have a site? Tune it instead." row. Door two: Four Gaps Audit, Territory Platform (featured), Operations Platform. Credit line under door two.

### 3. Homepage two-door section
Replaces the `statement` slot between hero and social proof. Door one links to /pricing and /work; door two links to /territory. The line for door two: *"For companies that built their name over decades, and refuse to rent it back one click at a time."*

### 4. Four Gaps Audit page
A paid discovery ($1,500 to $2,500): we read your ad platform, your inquiry database, and your search territory, and present the four gaps on your own numbers. Reuses the per-instance discovery form (`/discovery/<client>/`) as its intake; the data reading itself is manual analysis, which the page should not overstate. Signal's AI-engine reading is gap three. The About Signal intro repoints here.

### 5. Operations Platform shape doc, then the price
One page: multi-location operators, CRM backbone, revenue attribution, AI agents, customer portal. Written before "from $90,000" goes on the site.

### 6. One vertical page first: commercial door and dock distributors
"Websites for overhead door distributors," from the OHDBalt vocabulary research (spec, bid, drawing, dock, roll up). Doubles as the landing page for the post-live distributor outreach. Carries the exclusivity line. Replicate per trade later (commercial HVAC, roofing, electrical) only after this one proves.

### 7. Case study + Notes
Rewrite `/case-study-overhead-door/` into the platform story at launch, with permission. Notes articles from the findings, unnamed, each ending at the Four Gaps Audit: "Cost per lead quietly defunds your best customers" · "What nine years of pay-per-click leaves behind: nothing" · "Your reviews are invisible where customers actually look" · "Owned versus rented: the only distinction that matters in marketing spend."

### 8. Shared language bank
Reuse the deck's proven lines sitewide: owned versus rented · the content earns the visit, the website earns the call · available everywhere, required nowhere · a report must never look complete while it is not · it types, it never talks · nothing you rely on changes.
