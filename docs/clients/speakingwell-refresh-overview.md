## What's new, in plain terms

**Listening comes first.** The site now opens with Graham's message and a one-tap "Listen now." The new audio player works like Spotify: it sits at the bottom of the screen, keeps playing while people browse, remembers where they left off, and is a pleasure to use on a phone. Series pages even mark each talk with a small check when you've finished it. It's **our own player**, so there are no paid plugin fees, and it's light on data for listeners overseas.

**The audio library is organized the way the ministry thinks about it**, grouped by speaker (A. Graham Maxwell, Richard Nies, Jack Provonsha, Paul Jacobson), with each collection sitting under its author's name. Every talk now shows its **real title** instead of a file name, for example *"The Conflict in God's Family"* rather than *"cag_01."* We sourced those titles from Pine Knoll's own records so they're authentic, in both English and Spanish.

**A proper home for the videos.** The Vimeo teachings now live in a modern "Watch" section instead of a plain list.

**Search everything.** A new search in the top corner finds any series, talk, book, or chapter, in whichever language the visitor is browsing.

**Reading is a pleasure now.** The books have a real chapter picker, clean type sized for phones, and easy next/previous navigation. The site remembers where you stopped reading and offers to pick up right there, with a slim progress bar as you read.

**All eleven languages carried over**: same web addresses, no lost search rankings, and each language now gets the full new design. Arabic keeps its proper right-to-left layout, menus stay in your language, and every label (down to the player's buttons) is translated.

**The gift-book store is untouched** and keeps working exactly as before.

**Faster and steadier**, especially for the international audience. Since launch we've kept working on this. The pages are now stored ready-made instead of being rebuilt on every visit, and the shop's code no longer loads on pages that aren't the shop. A page that used to take about 1.19 seconds to start arriving now takes about 0.29, and the home page asks the browser for sixteen files instead of thirty-nine. Google's score on a phone is 96, with Accessibility, Best Practices and SEO all at 100.

We've done that **without buying a content delivery network**, the paid service most sites reach for when their audience is overseas. The work above achieved most of what one is bought for, at no monthly cost. It stays available later if you ever want the last portion of that distance closed.
