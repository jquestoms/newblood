## 1. Backstory — the company

**The parent.** Overhead Door Corporation, Lewisville TX. Founded 1921 in Detroit by C.G. Johnson — *invented* the upward-acting garage door (1921) and the first electric opener (1926). Tagline "The Genuine. The Original." Owned since 1996 by Sanwa Holdings (Tokyo, ~$470M acquisition). Distribution is through **400+ independent "Red Ribbon" distributors** across North America — the ribbon logo marks an authorized distributor. Corporate runs a **find-a-distributor locator** that has a dedicated page for OHDBalt (a lead channel you don't control — ask about it).

**The local company.** Overhead Door Company of Baltimore, Inc. — family-managed 50+ years (one directory says since 1967), **100+ employees**, HQ at 3501 Century Ave, Baltimore 21227 (Halethorpe/Lansdowne), plus **two Delaware branches: New Castle and Milford**. The numbers they brag on-site: 28 service trucks, ~9,000 service calls/year, 1,080-part inventory, many 30-year employees.

**The business is commercial-first.** Sectional, rolling steel, and high-speed doors; dock equipment (levelers, lifts, seals, restraints); motor operators; **fire door drop testing**; **planned maintenance programs**; 24/7 emergency service. Residential (traditional + carriage house + operators) exists but reads secondary. This matters: the generic discovery kit assumes a residential local-service business — OHDBalt's best growth levers are probably B2B.

**Your relationship.** You've managed the site for years on the $95/mo Web Care Plan. The stack is honest about its age: dfd-ronneby theme (c. 2014) + WPBakery + RevSlider, Forminator forms, ACF — and **no SEO plugin installed at all**. Core is current (the care plan working), but the visibility layer was never built. That's not a failure story — it's the setup for "the foundation has been maintained; it's never been *tuned*."

**The competitive field.** Residential repair in Baltimore is shark-infested: Precision Garage Doors of Baltimore, Champions, ADP, Eagle, New Look — review-heavy, ad-heavy. The **commercial/industrial side is much thinner**: Allmark Door (industrial, Baltimore→DC), Baltimore Precision Door, Bob's Overhead Door (Ellicott City — appears to be *another* Red Ribbon distributor; territory question worth asking). Niche commercial queries — "dock leveler repair Baltimore," "fire door drop test Maryland," "high speed door installation" — are low-competition, high-ticket, and OHDBalt already has the pages' raw material in its service list.

**Fire door drop testing is a compliance product.** NFPA 80 requires annual testing; hospitals/institutions are audited on it. Recurring, deadline-driven demand that almost nobody targets with content. Same for planned-maintenance contracts — the highest-LTV lead type they have.

**What the reviews actually say — the pattern is clean:**
- **The field operation gets praised.** Techs called ahead, arrived early, "professional and friendly," came back quickly for a follow-up issue, "good job for a good price" on full installs.
- **The complaints are almost all front-office, not workmanship:** the billing structure (1-hr labor minimum + 1-hr travel time + $9 fuel surcharge) **not disclosed before booking** — one customer billed ~$180 for a 5-minute sensor adjustment; a customer-service manager described as "appallingly unsympathetic and robot-like"; one serious allegation of a **forged signature on a work order** with no documentation sent. No evidence anyone from the company responds to any review, anywhere.

**Why this is good news for the pitch:** the product is fine — the *expectation-setting* is broken, and that's a digital fix:
1. **A transparent service-call pricing page** (minimum, travel, surcharge, what to expect) pre-frames every booking, cuts the angriest review category off at the source — and doubles as an SEO asset ("garage door service call cost Baltimore").
2. **A review-response protocol** — the forged-signature accusation has sat unanswered in public; a measured owner response is reputation triage.
3. **The capture-rate machine** — 9,000 jobs/yr funneling even 2% into Google reviews buries the back-catalog of complaints within two quarters.

**Entity fragmentation (clarify on the call):** corporate lists the New Castle and Milford addresses as *separate distributorships* — "Overhead Door Co. of Northern Delaware" and "Overhead Door Co. of DelMar" (which runs its own site, overheaddoordelmar.com) — while ohdbalt.com presents them as branches. Same family? Separate entities under common ownership? This determines whether the program manages one entity or three, and explains why Google's understanding of "who is this company" is likely muddled.

## 2c. The fire-door proof point — and the hijacked phone number (added 2026-06-10, eve of call)

Jeremy spot-checked **"fire door drop testing baltimore md"**: OHDBalt is **#1 in organic AND cited first in Google's AI Overview** (and well-positioned on Gemini) — the AI quotes their own 2019 fire-door page ("annual compliance requirement mandated by NFPA 80"). With zero optimization. **This is the live proof that the niche-commercial content thesis works** — one real page in a thin niche owns the answer. Imagine it done deliberately for dock levelers, high-speed doors, planned maintenance, rolling steel.

**The (888) 594-0830 number — SOLVED (corrected 2026-06-10 evening).** Initial read ("lead-broker hijack") was wrong. It's **YoTrack dynamic number insertion (Natpal platform), installed by Jeremy himself in April 2021** for **Enspire for Enterprise** (contact then: Kristin Slocum), per email records. The script (`yotrack.cdn.ybn.io/yotrack_auto.min.js`) lives in the prod DB (theme custom-JS, not in files), swaps the displayed 410 number to Enspire's tracking line after page load, and forwards callers to the real business. It **also pipes Forminator form submissions to Enspire** via a `submitWebLead()` hook. Not a hack; an attribution product someone has presumably been paying for since 2021.

**The real risks (these survive the correction):**
1. **Google's AI indexed the tracking line as their public phone number.** JS number-swapping leaks: the AI Overview now publishes Enspire's 888 line, which conflicts with GBP/citations showing 410-636-6300 — a self-inflicted NAP inconsistency, machine-amplified.
2. **Enspire currently claims attribution for OHDBalt's inbound calls AND web leads.** Any SEO program's results would flow through a *third party's* measurement — and that vendor has every incentive to credit its own campaigns. Reporting integrity question.
3. **If the Enspire campaign is no longer active**, the swap is pure downside: wrong number in AI answers, leads reported to a vendor nobody's paying attention to.

**Ask Chase tomorrow (genuinely curious tone, not gotcha — Jeremy installed it at their request):**
- Is the **Enspire for Enterprise** relationship still active? What is it — directory ads, lead-gen, media buy? What does it cost?
- What does Enspire report monthly, and does anyone reconcile their claimed call/lead numbers against reality?
- Does Chase know the website swaps its displayed phone number for Enspire's tracking line — and that Google's AI now shows that 888 number?

**The revised call moment:** "Google's AI already recommends you first for fire door testing — and the phone number it publishes is your ad vendor's tracking line, not yours. Do you know what Enspire's reporting claims about those calls?" Still a moment nobody else quoting Chase can produce — and it sets up clean measurement (call tracking *you* control, reconciled reporting) as part of the program.

**Removal note:** if they ever want it gone, it's the script tag in the prod theme custom-JS field + the Forminator `submitWebLead` hooks — but do NOT remove unilaterally; it may be tied to an active ad contract.

**Separate, still-unexplained:** garagedoorrepair.com (a lead-gen directory) lists them under **(410) 773-9711** — possibly that directory's own tracking overlay, possibly another Enspire line. Minor; the citation-cleanup pass handles it.

Also visible in the same AI answer: **Mid-Atlantic Door Group** (overheaddoors.com) — *another* Overhead Door Red Ribbon distributor serving Maryland — sharing the citation block. Territory blur inside AI answers; one more reason for the corporate/territory question.

## 3. The customized question line

**Opening (relationship + why now)**
- "We've kept this site healthy for a decade — what's making digital presence a priority *now*?" (new competitor? slow segment? Chase's own initiative? corporate nudge?)
- What does Chase's role cover — Baltimore only, or the Delaware branches too?

**Business mix & goals (the big one — replaces the generic "goals" section)**
- Of the three engines — **commercial install, service/planned maintenance, residential** — which do you most want to grow? Which is most profitable?
- What's a planned-maintenance contract worth over its life? A fire-door testing client? An average commercial install? (anchors the ROI math far higher than residential tickets)
- With 28 trucks, how much more service volume could you absorb before hiring?
- Do you *want* residential leads, or is residential mostly brand presence?

**Lead flow today**
- Where do leads come from now — corporate's find-a-distributor page, word of mouth, repeat contracts, the website form? Rough split?
- Who reads websiteInquiry@? What happens to a form fill at 2pm? At 2am (you advertise 24/7)?
- Any call tracking? Do you know how many calls the website produces monthly?

**The corporate relationship (unique to this client — ask all of these)**
- Does Overhead Door corporate provide **co-op marketing funds** for distributors? (Many networks reimburse 50% of approved local marketing — this could halve the program's effective cost)
- Brand rules: any constraints on local content, keywords, or pages?
- Does corporate run ads or SEO in your territory? Do you know how Bob's Overhead Door (Ellicott City) territory relates to yours?

**Customers (commercial framing)**
- Top 3 commercial customer types? (warehouse/distribution, property management, healthcare, government, institutions)
- Who *calls* you — facility managers, GCs, purchasing? What do they type when the dock door is stuck and trucks are waiting?
- Fire-door testing: who buys it, and is it sold proactively or when their auditor flags them?

**Reviews & reputation (the fastest win — set it up here)**
- Walk me through job close-out: does the tech use software? Is there any post-job text/email today? (9,000 jobs = 9,000 review chances; 59 lifetime reviews)
- Who, if anyone, responds to Google reviews now? Is anyone watching the pricing complaints?

**Delaware**
- How do New Castle and Milford get business? Do they have their own Google Business Profiles? Growth ambitions there, or steady-state?

**Modern layer (the differentiator moment)**
- "Have you ever asked ChatGPT who to call for commercial garage door repair in Baltimore?" — consider doing it live on the call; whoever it names, that's the conversation.

**Decision & measurement**
- Family-managed: besides Chase, who signs off?
- What single number tells you this worked — service calls? Maintenance contracts signed? Commercial quote requests?

## 4. Pricing posture for THIS client

The program doc anchors $3,500 sweep + from $950/mo for a typical single-market local business. OHDBalt is **bigger**: 100+ employees, 3 locations, 2 states, B2B + B2C. Position $950 as the *Baltimore-market starting scope*; Delaware branches and the commercial content program would scope higher (realistically $1,500–2,500/mo if they want all three locations worked). Let the sweep's baseline set it — that's the doc's own logic. The commercial job values (a single dock-equipment contract or maintenance agreement) make the ROI math trivial.

## 5. Things NOT to lead with

- **The site rebuild.** The 10-year-old WPBakery stack will eventually be the ceiling — the sweep's audit will surface that honestly, and a Build conversation can follow later. Leading with "your site is old" undercuts a decade of your own stewardship; leading with "the foundation is solid and maintained — now we tune it for how people search today" honors it.
- **The 3.8 rating.** Frame reviews as "your service volume is a massive untapped asset," never as criticism of their operation.
- The audit gaps (no schema, no meta) — these are *your* proof points for the sweep, mentioned as opportunity, not as a list of what's wrong.
